## 5 · Targets

| Milestone | Conservative | Target | Stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 1 — Oct | 0–2 | **3** | 5 |
| Month 3 — Dec | 4 | **8** | 12 |
| Month 6 — Mar 2027 | 10 | **18** | 26 |
| Month 9 — Jun 2027 | 18 | **30** | 42 |
| Month 12 — Sep 2027 | 25 | **45** | 65 |

**A qualified organic lead** means all four: it came from organic search, a Google Business
Profile action or an AI answer; you have a name and a working phone number; they named a service
or a problem; they're in the service area or deliverable remotely. Everything else is traffic.

**What is not promised:** a position, a date for a position, or a number of sales. Three to six
months for meaningful movement; longer on competitive terms. The head term "digital marketing
agency in Pondicherry" is a six-to-twelve month job against entrenched incumbents.

## 6 · Reporting

**Monthly, by the fifth working day.** Six blocks, same shape every month:

1. **Headline** — qualified organic leads this month vs last vs baseline. One number, at the top.
2. **What we did** — countable deliverables against what the plan promised
3. **What moved** — tracked rankings, non-brand clicks, impressions, GBP calls and direction requests
4. **AI visibility** — prompt panel: named in how many of twenty, who else was named
5. **What's blocked** — what we need, from whom, by when
6. **Next month** — three specific actions

**Weekly:** Friday audit, 30 minutes, fixed agenda. Six-line summary posted to the group.

**Never lead with rankings.** A client whose rankings rose and whose phone stayed quiet will
churn, and they'd be right to.

## 8 · Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Client work eats content time | **High** | Ring-fence Monday brief and Tue–Wed draft blocks as unbookable. Two clashes in a month → formally drop to fortnightly rather than miss targets silently. |
| Karthika is a single point of failure | **High** | She's in every row of this plan, for BM TechX and every paying client. Document the briefs and the optimisation checklist so a second person can execute, or accept a slower cadence — decide before week 4. |
| Cancelled in month two | Medium | Agree in writing now: judged on leading indicators until month 3, on leads from month 3. |
| Dev tickets never ship | Medium | D1–D4 are blockers. Ring-fence dev days or buy four weeks of freelance front-end. |
| Leads arrive and nobody answers fast | Medium | First response inside 15 minutes in hours, 1 hour overnight. This is what the autopilot layer exists to guarantee. |

## 9 · Reusing this plan for a client

The structure transfers unchanged. What must be rebuilt per client:

- **Sections 2, 3 and 4** — their audit, their keywords, their deliverables at *their* tier
  (Starter gets 2 pages + 1 article/month, not 8 and 4)
- **Section 5** — scale the targets to their plan and market
- **Section 7** — what you need from *them*

What stays identical: the reporting format, the honesty about what isn't promised, the risk
section, and the rule that the baseline is exported on day one before anything changes.

For The Balified Villa (Growth, 10 keywords, 31 Aug – 22 Nov), build the first report in this
exact shape, have it reviewed before it goes out, and make it the template for every client
after — including whoever takes the account over.
